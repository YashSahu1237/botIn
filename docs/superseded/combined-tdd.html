Part A

Executive summary

Written to stand alone. A reader who stops at the end of Part A should be able to approve, reject, or send back this proposal without reading further.

A.1The problem

Every Partner Support query today is answered by a human agent. Most of them do not need a human.

In the May sample, 6,245 tickets — 12.46% of all volume — were one question: why was I fined for taking leave? The three largest concerns together account for 54.4% of volume. These are not conversations requiring judgement. Each is a lookup against data we already hold, followed by a decision that follows a written SOP, followed by a credit to a wallet.

Concern volume, May sample. Source: Ticket Flow Automation — Partner Support (SOP workbook).
ConcernShare of volumeWaveDisposition
Rule-break fine30.2%1Automate
Leaves fine12.5%1Automate — POC subject
Transport amount12.0%1Automate
Remaining ~27 L2 concerns45.6%2–4Waves 2–3 automate; Wave 4 stays human

Agent time spent on the first three rows is the cost this project removes. Strike appeals — deliberately placed in Wave 4 — stay with humans, because they are genuine judgement calls and automating them would be the wrong kind of efficiency.

A.2What we propose

BOTIn Mod puts a bot in front of every Partner Support query and hands to a human only when a defined trigger fires. Four tiers, decided per request at runtime:

TierBehaviourTicket createdExample
T0 DeflectAnswer from static content; conversation endsNo"Where do I see my payout date?"
T1 Auto-resolveRead our own data, state the answerYes"There is no fine on that date."
T2 Auto-actionRead data, apply a rule, move moneyYesRefund a leave fine
T3 Agent connectAssemble context, call the partner, hand to an agentYesLow confidence, or a judgement concern

The bot is not a chatbot in the conversational sense. It is a server-driven flow in which every step is a menu, a database read, a rule evaluation, or an action. A language model is used in exactly one place — turning free-typed Hinglish into one value from a fixed list. It never decides anything, never writes a message the partner reads, and structurally cannot move money (§4, ADR-003 and ADR-004).

A.3Goals and non-goals

Goals

  • Resolve the Wave 1 concerns without an agent, at the BRD's target of 86% bot-handled / 10–20% agent-connect.
  • Make every automated money movement auditable, idempotent, and individually reversible by a switch an ops user can flip in seconds.
  • Keep the business rules editable by the people who own them, without a code deploy.
  • Hand escalated partners to an agent who already has the context, rather than one who starts by asking what happened.

Non-goals

  • A general-purpose assistant. The taxonomy is closed: ~30 L2 concerns from the SOP. Anything outside it goes to a human.
  • LLM-authored partner-facing text. Responses are reviewed templates. See ADR-004 for why, and what that costs us.
  • Replacing agents on judgement concerns. Strike appeals stay human by design, not by omission.
  • New paid infrastructure. The workflow engine is a library inside our own service, not a platform to operate.
  • Changing fine or leave policy. This system automates the SOP as written. If the SOP is wrong, that is a separate conversation.

Part B

Engineering design

The architecture, the decisions behind it with the alternatives that were rejected, and the mechanisms a reviewer should test hardest.

1Context and current state

Today a Service Partner raising a query in the partner app reaches a human agent. There is no self-service layer, no automated resolution, and no structured capture of why the partner got in touch — the reason exists only as free text in a ticket and in the agent's head.

Three repositories are relevant, and the distinction matters because it constrains where anything can live:

RepositoryAudienceRelevance
empapiService PartnersWhere partner identity, wallet, leave and fines live. The only viable host if we do not build standalone, and the target of every Layer 6 call either way
adminInternal agentsRenders the agent screen. A UI concern — not a backend host candidate
app_serverCustomersRuled out. Wrong audience entirely

External reference: Swiggy's published account of their enterprise support agent is the closest public analogue, and its central finding shaped ADR-003 — end-to-end LLM routing plateaued around 90% accuracy for them, and they improved on it by combining rule-based routing with one small model doing one narrow job. That is the boundary this design draws.

2Solution overview

Every request follows the same seven-step chain, regardless of concern. What differs between concerns is configuration, not code path.

Tier is decided at step 5, at runtime, by the decision table — it is not a fixed property of a concern. The same concern can resolve at T1 for one partner and escalate to T3 for another, because the facts differ. This is a deliberate departure from the BRD's Table 9, which assigns a tier per concern; the two are reconcilable if Table 9 is read as the expected majority outcome rather than a constraint (open question O-4, §13).

Where the language model fits

The menu path needs no AI at all. Free text appears in exactly two places, and both are the same problem — turn unconstrained human language into one value from a fixed list:

  • Entry with no selection. The partner opens Help and types "mera aaj strike laga hai usko hatao" instead of tapping a menu. Nothing downstream can start, because a process is selected by name and we have no name yet. Handled by /classify-intent against the whole taxonomy.
  • The reason step. Within a known concern, the partner explains why. Handled by /classify against that one concern's fixed category list.

These are different jobs at different scales, which is why they are separate endpoints rather than a mode flag. Consequently, Python is called based on input shape, not on tier. T0 and T1 never call it. A fully menu-driven T2 session calls it zero times.

4Architecture decision records

Eight decisions that shape everything else. Each records what was rejected and what the choice costs — a decision presented without its cost is not a decision, it is advocacy.

ADR-001Embedded BPMN engine (Flowable) for orchestrationDecided
Context
T0, T1 and T2 could be plain Java — request in, response out. T3 cannot. It pauses at "waiting for an agent" and must resume hours later, across deploys and restarts, without losing where it was.
Options
Hand-rolled state machine — no dependency, but we write and debug persistence, resumption, retry and timer semantics ourselves. Temporal — purpose-built and excellent, but a server to operate and a new paid platform; rejected — it adds infrastructure to run, which this design explicitly avoids. Flowable, embedded — a Maven dependency running in our JVM, persisting to the database we already have.
Decision
Flowable, embedded. On startup it creates roughly 25 ACT_* tables and scans src/main/resources/processes/*.bpmn20.xml. Each Service Task calls a Spring bean via flowable:delegateExpression="${beanName}". "Route to Agent 2" is a native User Task with queue semantics, not something we build.
Consequences
The diagram is the executable, so the flow can be reviewed by someone who does not read Java. In exchange: ~25 tables in our schema, a new notation to author and review, and in-flight instances pinned to the old process version across a deploy. History level is set to AUDIT, not FULL, to bound write volume.
ADR-002Business rules as DMN tables, not Java conditionalsDecided
Context
The "is this a genuine case?" rules will be tuned repeatedly — thresholds move, medal bands get exceptions, new reason categories appear. Routing every tuning cycle through a code deploy puts engineering in the path of a business decision.
Options
Java if chains — fastest to write, invisible to ops, needs a deploy per change. BPMN gateways — fine for two or three branches, unreadable as a rule grid. DMN decision tables — a grid of conditions and outcomes, and already available: Flowable's DMN engine ships in the same dependency as the process engine, so this costs no new library.
Decision
One DMN table per automated concern, hit policy FIRST, bound from the BPMN diagram by a single Business Rule Task. Cells use FEEL — >= 0.7, <= 2, "TITANIUM","QUEEN" — which is deliberately readable without Java.
Consequences
Editing one cell and redeploying the .dmn file changes behaviour with no recompile. That is the benefit and the hazard: these rows move money. Row order is semantically significant under FIRST, so a misordered catch-all silently swallows specific rules. Requires the review gate in D-3 before Wave 1.
ADR-003A separate Python service for language understandingDecided
Context
Partners write in mixed Hindi, English and transliterated Hinglish, in one sentence. Keyword matching is brittle against this; classical NLU needs labelled training data we do not have; an LLM works from a described category list with no training data at all.
Options
An LLM client inside the Java service — one deployable, simpler ops. A separate Python service — Pydantic response validation and mature vendor SDKs, independent deploy cadence, and a physical safety boundary.
Decision
A small FastAPI service with two endpoints and no database credentials. Called with a 3-second timeout behind a Resilience4j circuit breaker.
Consequences
The classifier can misclassify but cannot wrongly credit a wallet, because it structurally cannot reach one. That is a real property, not a diagram aesthetic. Stated honestly: a well-isolated Java package would achieve most of the same isolation. This choice is defensible, not a slam dunk, and it costs one more deployable to operate.
ADR-004Partner-facing responses are templates, never generatedDecided
Context
Once a case is resolved, something has to tell the partner. The obvious question is whether the model already in the stack should write that message.
Options
Templates keyed by the DMN action output. Model localises a template at runtime. Model generates freely. Model drafts translations at design time, a human reviews and commits them.
Decision
Templates, plus design-time model-assisted translation with human review. Language selection is solved by classification, not generation: /classify returns detectedLanguage and Java picks the matching template. Python understands language; Java produces it.
Consequences
Rules out hallucinated amounts and dates in financial messages, unauthorised commitments, and non-deterministic text in a dispute. A template can be reviewed before it is sent; generated text only after. It also keeps T0 and T1 at zero model calls. What we give up is naturalness — a real cost, and a smaller one than a wrong number in a message about money. Roughly 30 concerns × 3–4 outcomes ≈ 100 messages: a content problem, not a generation problem.
ADR-005The action record commits in its own transactionDecided
Context
A Flowable Service Task runs inside the engine's transaction. If a later step in the same transaction throws, everything rolls back — including our record that we called an external system. The external system does not roll back with it.
Options
Accept the default transaction — simplest, and the failure mode is money moved with no trace of it. Write the record after the call succeeds — narrows the window, does not close it. @Transactional(propagation = REQUIRES_NEW) on the attempt record, committed before the external call.
Decision
TicketActionRecorder.recordAttempt() is annotated REQUIRES_NEW and commits an ATTEMPTED row before the outbound call is made.
Consequences
A rollback can now leave an ATTEMPTED row with no completion — which is exactly right: reconciliation can see it and resolve it. The alternative loses the evidence entirely. This single annotation is the most load-bearing line in the design, which is why POC Phase 8 tests it deliberately rather than trusting it.
ADR-006concern_catalogue as the single source of the taxonomyDecided
Context
The L1/L2 concern list is consumed in at least six places: the client menu, process selection, fact fetching, decision table binding, the kill switch, and wave planning. Six copies of a list is six chances to drift.
Options
A Java enum — type-safe, needs a deploy to change, invisible to ops. Config files — editable, no referential integrity. A database table whose columns are pointers to the other five artifacts.
Decision
One table. Beyond labels and ordering it carries process_key → Flowable, fact_provider → the ConcernFactProvider bean, dmn_key → the decision table, togglz_flag → the kill switch, plus default_tier and wave.
Consequences
One generic fetchFactsDelegate serves all ~30 concerns by resolving a provider from the row. But adding an L2 concern is never a pure config change — the row is only useful once the BPMN process, the fact provider bean, the DMN table and the templates exist. Saying otherwise would set a false expectation with ops. See §10.
ADR-007Kill switches via Togglz, with fail-to-human semanticsDecided
Context
The gap between noticing that money is moving wrongly and stopping it. Via revert-rebuild-redeploy that gap is 15–30 minutes of continued incorrect payments.
Options
A config flag with a restart — minutes, and a restart under incident conditions. A database column checked in code — works, hand-built, no console. Togglz — a Spring-native library, no server: a Feature enum, a JDBCStateRepository, and a console at /togglz-console.
Decision
Togglz, one flag per automated concern, checked after the decision table returns auto-approve and before the action executor runs. Gradual percentage rollout is built in, which is the BRD's ramp requirement for free.
Consequences
The critical semantic: flag off does not mean fail — it means do not automate, send to a human (T3). The partner still gets service. Two known costs: concern_catalogue.togglz_flag is a runtime string against an enum-based API, resolved with new NamedFeature(...), which bypasses enum type safety and needs startup validation; and JDBCStateRepository stores current state only — not who flipped it or when. Flip auditing must be added before Wave 1 (D-7).
ADR-008Tier is a runtime outcome, not a property of a concernDecided
Context
The BRD's Table 9 assigns a tier to each concern. But two partners raising the identical concern can warrant different treatment — one has a clean history and a clear fact pattern, the other has three similar claims this month.
Options
Fixed tier per concern — simple, and forces the automation threshold to the most cautious case, discarding most of the benefit. Tier as a decision-table output, computed per request from the facts.
Decision
tier is an output column of the DMN table, alongside action. concern_catalogue.default_tier exists only as a fallback when no table is configured.
Consequences
Automation rate becomes a tunable rather than a fixed property, and medal-band differentiation is one table row rather than a code branch. It also means no static document can state the tier of a concern — including this one. Any conflict with BRD Table 9 should be read as Table 9 describing the expected majority outcome (O-4, §13).

Six tables. Layer 2 is a package, not a service — zero HTTP endpoints and zero outbound calls, which makes it a leaf in the dependency graph and safe for everything else to depend on.

Two entities, not one, for a reason. A Help interaction exists before a ticket does. A T0 deflection creates a help_session and never creates a ticket — which is the BRD's Gate 1 made literal, and the reason T0 volume does not appear in ticket counts at all.

Write criticality is tiered deliberately. Four of the six are on the request path and must be synchronous. conversation_message is the highest-volume write and the least critical — it is the raw transcript, deliberately not what an agent sees first — so it is published as an event after commit and written on a separate thread. That keeps transcript volume off the partner's response latency without risking the records that matter.

Index required before Wave 1: ticket(sp_id, created_at). The claim-frequency read sits on the critical path of every T2 decision.

The client contract

Every response carries a nextStep descriptor — menu, dropdown, text, message or csat — plus its options or content. The backend owns 100% of the flow logic; the client is a thin renderer. The alternative puts flow logic in two places, where it drifts.

One mechanism worth stating because it is a real trap: after startProcessInstanceByKey(...) returns, Layer 1 must not read process variables. A completed process instance has its runtime row deleted, so those variables are gone. The process's final Service Task writes nextStep onto the help_session row, and Layer 1 re-reads it from there.

The agent handoff — two calls, unequal weight

GET /tickets/{id}/escalation-context is called first, before the call even connects, and returns computed facts: category, confidence, the already-extracted reason, medal band, a recent-history rollup, prior actions, and which trigger fired. GET /tickets/{id}/conversation is secondary and lazy — behind a "view full conversation" control, not auto-loaded.

Deliberately not built: a model call to write a prose summary for the agent. extractedReason already is the summary, structured rather than prose, reused from the classification that already ran. A third model touchpoint to re-summarise what has already been extracted is scope creep, not a missing piece.

7Runtime trace — a T2 leave-fine refund

The highest-stakes path, traced end to end. Every step names what it receives, what it does, and what it passes on.

  1. SP app → POST /help/sessionsPartner taps Help and selects Leave fine. help_session row created. No ticket yet.
  2. Gate 1 — ticket createdAn L2 concern needing an action was selected, so a ticket row is inserted. From here everything is attributable to a ticket id.
  3. Catalogue lookupOne row of concern_catalogue yields process_key=leave-fine, fact_provider=LEAVE_FINE, dmn_key=leave-fine-genuine, togglz_flag=leave-fine-auto-waive.
  4. Client renders the fine list, partner picks one, then types a reasonTwo more /input calls. The free text lands as a process variable.
  5. Flowable Service Task → ClassifierClient → POST /classify3-second timeout behind a circuit breaker. Returns {category, confidenceScore, extractedReason, detectedLanguage}. On timeout, non-2xx, or an open circuit, tier is set to T3 and the flow continues — it never blocks on a flaky dependency.
  6. Service Task → fetchFactsDelegate → LeaveFineFactProviderReal database reads: fineExists, fineAmount, fineAgeDays, alreadyRefunded, leaveBalance, claimFreq30d, medalBand.
  7. Business Rule Task → DMN engineThe facts and the category go in as a map; {tier, action} comes out. Synchronous, in-process, sub-millisecond. This is the only bridge between the process engine and the decision engine.
  8. Exclusive Gateway on ${tier}T2 continues to the action path. T1 goes to a message. T3 goes to the escalation path.
  9. Gateway on the Togglz flagIf leave-fine-auto-waive is off, the flow routes to T3 — a human, not an error.
  10. recordAttempt() — REQUIRES_NEW, commits firstAn ATTEMPTED ticket_action row with idempotencyKey = hash(ticketId, actionType) is committed before any external call. ADR-005 in one line.
  11. FineActionService.refund(...)The money moves. Same idempotency key on retry, so a duplicate is a no-op rather than a double credit.
  12. Record the outcome, select a templateticket_action updated to SUCCEEDED. The template is chosen by DMN action + detectedLanguage.
  13. finalizeDelegate writes nextStep to help_sessionNot to process variables — those vanish on completion (§6).
  14. Client polls or re-reads, renders the message, then CSATTicket closed. Total model calls in this path: one.

8Money safety

T2 moves money with no human in the loop. Four independent controls, each covering a failure the others do not.

The hazard, stated plainly. A Flowable Service Task runs inside the engine's transaction. If a step after the external call throws, the engine rolls back — and the rollback erases our record that we called the external system. The external system does not roll back. The result is money moved and no evidence in our database that we moved it. Not a lost record: a financial reconciliation failure with no audit trail.

Independent transaction@Transactional(propagation = REQUIRES_NEW) on recordAttempt(), committed before the outbound callThe hazard above. A rollback leaves an ATTEMPTED row that reconciliation can see and resolve
Kill switchTogglz flag per concern, checked after the decision and before the actionSystematic error discovered in production. Seconds to stop, and partners still get served — by a human
ReconciliationA Spring @Scheduled job summing ticket_action credits per partner against the actual ledgerEverything the first three miss. Alerts on any mismatch — target is zero, so any non-zero is actionable

Retry and idempotency are a designed pair, not two features. Flowable will retry a failed job; the idempotency key is what makes that retry safe. Neither is sound without the other, and reviewing one without the other misses the point.

POC Phase 8 tests the second control by deliberately constructing the failure: record the attempt, simulate a successful external call, throw from a later delegate, then assert that the Flowable transaction rolled back and the ticket_action row survived. If that test fails, the money-safety model needs redesign and the project stops there rather than continuing.

10What is code and what is configuration

The split is deliberate and worth stating precisely, because it sets expectations about who can change what without engineering.

What data to fetch is code. What to conclude from it is configuration. A fact provider is a Java bean because fetching correctly is an engineering concern. A decision table is a grid because concluding correctly is a business concern that will be tuned.

The last row is the one to be honest about. concern_catalogue makes a concern configurable, not free — the row is a set of pointers, and the things it points at have to exist. Presenting it as "just add a row" would set an expectation with ops that the design cannot meet.

Where response templates live and who owns editing them is not yet designed. A messages/ resource bundle keyed by action and language is the obvious answer, but it is an assumption, not a decision — see D-4.

11Non-functional concerns

Observability Designed

Micrometer instrumented at each layer, exported via the OpenTelemetry Java agent into Prometheus and Grafana — or whatever the EKS cluster already runs, which is worth confirming before standing anything up (O-9, §13). Dashboards carry the KPIs from §A.7 with the alert thresholds attached.

Access control Not designed — D-7

Deliberately marked as a gap rather than hand-waved. The unresolved questions are concrete: who may flip a kill switch; who may edit a decision table that moves money; who may read a partner's transcript, which contains whatever they typed; and how admin authenticates to the BOTIn backend across origins. This must land before Wave 1 go-live, not after. Shipping a money-moving system with an unauthenticated /togglz-console is not acceptable, and the current design has no answer for it.

LLM guardrails Not designed

The classifier returns a category string that selects a code path. Nothing today validates that the returned category is a member of the expected enum, and nothing constrains what a prompt-injected free-text field could elicit. The mitigation is structural rather than clever — Python holds no credentials and can take no action (ADR-003) — so the blast radius is a misclassification, which routes to a human. Explicit validation still needs designing.

Data retention

conversation_message is the highest-volume table and holds whatever partners type, which may include personal information. Retention, archival and a PII policy are open (O-10, §13). Flowable history is capped at AUDIT level rather than FULL to bound its own growth.

12Delivery plan

Phase 1 — the proof of concept

One concern, carried end to end through every module the design needs. It exists to answer five questions that would be expensive to get wrong later. It is not a small version of the product — it is the shortest path that exercises Flowable, the DMN engine, the classifier, the action executor and the transaction boundary at once.

#QuestionAnswered byIf the answer is bad
U-1Does Flowable really pause at a User Task, survive a JVM restart, and resume?POC Phase 8 — the restart testStop. The entire justification for ADR-001 collapses
U-2Does the model reach usable accuracy on real Hinglish?POC Phase 10 — 150–200 hand-labelled real ticketsRaise the threshold and accept a higher agent-connect rate, or make free text a fallback rather than a front door
U-3Does REQUIRES_NEW really protect the action record through a rollback?POC Phase 8 — the rollback testStop. The money-safety model needs redesign
U-4Do the leave and fine capabilities exist as callable methods in empapi?POC Phase 0 — an audit of the codebase, no code writtenNot a blocker. Layer 6 becomes a build rather than an integration; nothing above the interface changes
U-5Can a DMN edit change behaviour with no redeploy?POC Phase 11ADR-002's main benefit is lost; rules become an engineering task

Exit criteria are agreed before the numbers are known, which is the only way a threshold means anything: ≥ 90% classifier accuracy means the design holds; 75–90% means proceed with a raised threshold and a higher expected agent-connect rate; below 75% means rethink the approach. Either safety test failing means stop.

One dependency worth naming: POC Phases 1–8 depend on nothing from Phase 0 except the test environment, so the data-labelling work is independent of the build. That labelling is also the step most likely to be dropped — and without it, Phase 10 produces an impression rather than a number, which forfeits the whole reason for running U-2.

Phases 2 onward

The shape of Phase 2 depends on U-4. An integration and a build are materially different pieces of work in Layer 6, and the rest of the phase cannot be scoped meaningfully until that audit returns.

The distinction reviewers most often collapse: the process engine remembers, the decision engine does not. The process engine is stateful — it writes ACT_RU_* rows, waits indefinitely, and survives restarts. The DMN engine is a stateless pure function: facts in, {tier, action} out. They ship in the same dependency and meet at exactly one place, the Business Rule Task.
